What it doesBrushForge turns a simple ownership list into a working constraint: search the catalogue, record what you have, and prefer available paints when you are choosing a substitute or planning the next recipe.

Collection data should save a decision

A tracker earns its place at the painting desk when it answers a practical question: “Do I own this?”, “Which close colour do I already have?”, or “What should I add to the next shopping list?” BrushForge keeps those decisions next to the paint catalogue rather than in an isolated spreadsheet.

  • Connect paints to recipes and project notes.

A collection workflow that stays useful

  1. Normalize as you add. Prefer catalogue entries with a brand, range, and product code over free-text names.
  2. Separate owned from wanted. A wishlist is a decision queue; it should not inflate what you can actually use today.
  3. Resolve duplicates deliberately. Two pots may be intentional backups, old/new formulas, or accidental purchases.
  4. Use the list in context. When a recipe asks for another brand, check your closest owned same-type paint before buying.
  5. Review after a project. Mark empty, replaced, or retired paints while the information is fresh.

Limits and data safety

The free tier currently supports up to 40 paints. Collections sync automatically between devices on the same platform, with manual backup and restore also available. Android and iOS do not have complete cross-platform sync, and a manual transfer between them may be incomplete. Verify the import and backup tools shown in your installed version before relying on them as your only copy.

Keep a recoverable source during migrationDo not delete a spreadsheet, tracker export, or photo record until the imported count and a representative set of brand/range/code combinations have been checked.
