What it doesA BrushForge recipe records the paints and sequence you want to repeat. Projects give that information a home for a particular miniature, unit, or scheme, while the converter helps when an original paint is unavailable.

Document decisions, not just colours

“Blue armour” is difficult to reproduce six months later. “Dark blue base, two thin coats; cooler midtone; edge highlight mixed 2:1” is actionable. A useful recipe preserves order, role, and technique alongside the catalogue paint.

When you translate a recipe, BrushForge gives you colour candidates. You still decide whether a base paint, wash, glaze, metallic, or transparent one-coat product can perform the original job.

A repeatable structure

Record four things for every step

1

Role

Base, shade, layer, glaze, edge highlight, effect, or another job in the sequence.

2

Paint

Record the brand, range, paint name, and code where available. That is enough to disambiguate repeated names.

3

Application

Thinning, number of coats, placement, brush or airbrush, and the undercoat beneath it.

4

Result

What changed after drying, and what you would adjust on the next model.

Translate the role before the name

Start by marking the non-negotiable role of each paint. For an opaque basecoat, coverage and finish may outweigh a tiny ΔE advantage. For a glaze or wash, transparency and flow are part of the colour result. For a metallic, particle size and sheen can dominate the comparison.

Use ΔE ≤5 as a shortlist when possible, not a promise. Paint a small test over the intended primer, let it dry, and evaluate it under the light where the miniature will be viewed.

Current free limits

The free tier supports up to five recipes and one project. Those limits are useful for trying a complete workflow before deciding whether you need more. Recipes and projects sync automatically between devices on the same platform, with manual backup and restore also available. Android and iOS do not have complete cross-platform sync, and manual transfers between them may be incomplete.

Practical project noteAdd the primer colour and varnish to the recipe. Both can materially change the apparent finish and value, yet both are easy to omit when a scheme is first written down.
