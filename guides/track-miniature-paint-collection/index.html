How to track a paint collection and stop buying duplicates

A useful inventory is more than a list of names. Give every paint a stable identity, current status, and shelf location. Then use ownership as a filter while converting recipes and shopping.

By Bas MahieuPublished 2 August 2026Updated 2 August 20268 minute read
Short answerTrack brand, range, paint name, product code, quantity, condition, status, and storage location. Keep “owned,” “wanted,” and “replace soon” separate. Review uncertain imports and repeated names manually, then check the collection before every purchase or recipe conversion.

1. Use a stable paint identity

Names alone create duplicates because the same words can appear in multiple brands, lines, or formula generations. In BrushForge’s public 10 July 2026 snapshot, 405 paint-name groups occur more than once, involving 1,024 catalogue records. Within individual brands, 296 groups still repeat.

Use the combination of brand, line, name, and code whenever available. Keep barcode as an additional lookup aid rather than assuming it will capture every regional or packaging change.

2. Separate ownership states

Use at least three states: owned, wishlist, and replace soon. If you mix those states in one undifferentiated list, a shop check can tell you that a paint exists without telling you whether it is actually on the shelf.

For intentional backups, keep one catalogue identity with a quantity rather than duplicating the row. Preserve separate rows only when the formula generation or range is meaningfully different.

3. Start with a shelf audit

  1. Work one rack, drawer, or brand at a time.
  2. Clean enough of the label to read the range and code.
  3. Add or confirm the catalogue identity.
  4. Mark quantity, condition, and location immediately.
  5. Put uncertain items in a review group instead of guessing.
  6. Compare the final count with the physical section before moving on.

A complete small section is more trustworthy than a rushed whole-room scan. The aim is a list you will believe while standing in a shop.

4. Migrate an existing tracker safely

A CSV or tracker export is easiest to reconcile when it includes brand, range, name, and code in separate fields. Before importing, keep an untouched copy. Afterward, compare total counts, inspect every unmatched row, and spot-check several common and unusual products.

Photo-assisted import can accelerate candidate identification, but labels can be hidden, redesigned, or confused with neighbouring pots. Confirm suggestions before accepting them. Import availability and accepted formats may differ by app version and platform; check the tools in your installed build before deleting the original tracker.

Cross-platform limitationData syncs automatically between devices on the same platform. Android and iOS do not have complete cross-platform sync, so do not assume an Android collection will appear automatically on iOS. A manual transfer may be incomplete. Keep a recoverable source while changing platforms.

5. Make the inventory part of painting

Update a paint when it enters or leaves the shelf, not during a large cleanup months later. Add new purchases before opening them. Mark a pot low when you notice it during a recipe. Review status at the end of a project while the materials are still on the desk.

The most valuable use comes during conversion: filter a shortlist to close same-type paints you already own. Avoiding one unnecessary purchase is more useful than maintaining a collection for its own sake.
